# Task 5 Answers (2026 Cohort) — Three Research Ideas

Three ideas with **different economic mechanisms, markets, data sources and horizons**:

- **Idea 1 — EU defense procurement** (event/disclosure data, structural re-rating, 6–18 months)
- **Idea 2 — Statistical pairs in EU airlines** (relative value, mean reversion, 1–15 trading days)
- **Idea 3 — Data-center power demand** (macro-to-sector demand pipeline, 3–12 months)

## Question 1: Research Idea Number One — EU Defense Procurement as a Leading Revenue Indicator

**1. Market and scope.**
European listed defense and defense-adjacent companies on Euronext/Xetra/Borsa Italiana/OMX: Rheinmetall (RHM.DE), Hensoldt (HAG.DE), Renk (R3NK.DE), Thales (HO.PA), Safran (SAF.PA), Dassault Aviation (AM.PA), Leonardo (LDO.MI), Indra (IDR.MC), Saab (SAAB-B.ST), Babcock International (BAB.L), plus large/mid-caps: BAE Systems (BA.L), Airbus (AIR.PA), Thyssenkrupp (TKA.DE), Fincantieri (FCT.MI). Horizon: 6–18 months per contract, portfolio rebalanced quarterly.

**2. Investment thesis.**
NATO members are contractually and politically committed to higher defense spend (2% GDP pledges, new capability targets, EU SAFE facility). Defense revenue is *contract-driven*: an awarded tender converts to backlog → deliveries → earnings only with 12–24 month lags. Public procurement in the EU/EEA must, above threshold (~€140k systems/services), be published on TED (Tenders Electronic Daily). So contract awards are **public, free, machine-readable, timestamped** — and most EU small/mid-cap names have little sell-side coverage collecting them. Thesis: cumulative per-company award value (normalized by market cap and trailing revenue) predicts revenue revisions and residual returns over the following 6–12 months, because only *part* of an award is priced at announcement and backlog announcements lag awards by quarters.

**3. Potential edge.**
The edge is *collection and normalization*, not secrecy. TED is in structured XML/JSON but not investor-friendly; aggregating per company requires a dedicated tickers-to-entity mapping table (Rheinmetall → "Rheinmetall AG" + subsidiaries "Rheinmetall Technical Publications", "WERKSTATT GMBH", etc.). Few systematic funds cover EU small/mid defense names deeply; retail flow focuses on the 5 megacaps. Interpretation skill: distinguish **framework contracts** (no volume guaranteed) from **specific lots**; distinguish awards to prime vs partner-company (subcontract share is real revenue but at lower margin).

**4. Data and sources.**

- **TED (europa.eu)** — Contracts/Awards notices XML feeds with buyer, CPV codes (35xx defense), value, winners. *Checked access: free bulk XML download + daily OJS feeds exist.* To verify: award-notice completeness across countries (many awards appear only as national gazette links).
- **SIPRI Military Expenditure Database + NATO press releases** — budget trajectories; lagged annually. *Checked.*
- **Company quarterly reports** for backlog/exists-order intake reconciliation (investor relations PDFs; to verify machine-readability).
- **Yahoo Finance EOD prices** for the listed names. *Checked (used throughout course).*
- Optional cross-check: **US DoD contract index** (api.data.gov) for EU companies' US revenue exposure. *To verify availability/vintage.*

**5. Research approach.**
Build a per-company award-intake series: `award_value_90d / market_cap` and `award_count_90d` per quarter. Signal = z-score of that series vs company's own 3-year history. Test: sort companies each quarter into quintiles by signal; forward 6-month returns of Q5–Q1; regress forward 12-month revenue growth on lagged award intensity (panel fixed effects). Also event-study of abnormal returns in the 20 days after large awards to estimate how much of the news is already discounted (informs whether trade is *only* the drift, or beat-the-headline pre-positioning). Decision: overweight Q5 names to 5–7% each inside a defense sleeve, rebalance quarterly.

**6. Validation and risks.**
Benchmark: STOXX Europe Aerospace & Defense index (net of fees) and equal-weight basket of the universe. Reject the idea if: (a) the award→return beta is not positive at 3–6 month horizons after pre-positioning window; (b) a paper-portfolio with T+1 execution after award notice, 10 bps costs, EUR/USD/SEK hedging ignored, fails to beat the index by >2–3% annualized; (c) award notices per company < ~20/year (signal too sparse for mid-caps). Other risks: headline-driven retail flows re-rates the whole sector (the whole thesis becomes a beta trade), tender values in local currencies, contract cancellations, and index-inclusion distortions (Rheinmetall's DAX/MSCI moves were price, not fundamentals).

## Question 2: Research Idea Number Two — Statistical Pairs: IAG vs Lufthansa

**1. Market and scope.**
One economically-linked equity pair in the same region and currency-basket: **IAG (IAG.L, London) vs Lufthansa (LHA.DE/FRA)** — both listed EU network carriers with intercontinental + intra-EU routes, jet-fuel exposure in USD, GBP/EUR revenue. Instruments traded on liquid ETPs/ADRs are unavailable for both; so legs traded in the primary listings with FX leg handled via GBP-EUR forward/futures (or by simply expressing in the same currency). Investment horizon per trade: 1–15 trading days; a rotating 2–3 pair basket.

**2. Investment thesis.**
Both stocks load on the *same* common factors (jet fuel, EUR demand cycle, USD strength, airline capacity, geo-shocks) and *different* idiosyncratic noise (strikes, IT outages, single-quarter PR issues, analyst upgrades). The spread between them should wander around a stable, slow-moving beta-adjusted mean; when it deviates by 2σ for reasons *not* traceable to a genuinely differentiating hard fact (e.g. one carrier discloses a structural cost fix), the gap closes in days as the common factor reasserts. Mechanism: shared economic driver, temporarily decoupled prices.

**3. Potential edge.**
Most funds run pair screens on US large caps; EU intra-sector pairs in a *cyclical commodity-cost sector* are less crowded, and retail flow overreacts to headline events (strike coverage) that hit only one leg. The interpretation requirement — first check *why* it diverged (USD-priced jet fuel while IAG earns GBP and LHA earns EUR, so GBP/EUR moves pass differently into each leg) — acts as a filter all systematic screens lack: veto entries whose divergence is explained by the FX leg, fuel spread, or a dated company-specific structural announcement.

**4. Data and sources.**

- **Yahoo Finance EOD** for IAG.L, LHA.DE (2005–present, split/dividend-adjusted). *Checked — daily data clean in our dataset.*
- **FX rates:** GBP/EUR + EUR/USD (ECB reference rates, FRED `EXCAUS`) — to hedge/purge FX effect. *Checked, free CSV.*
- **Jet fuel / WTI-Brent curve (EIA, FRED `DPROPANEMBTX` regional jet spot)** for the shared fuel factor. *Partially verified — check series continuity.*
- Company dailies: load factor/traffic releases (IAG monthly update, LHA traffic report) for veto list. *To verify publication timestamps.*

**5. Research approach.**
Rolling 252-day window per week: OLS spread `log(LHA) − β·log(IAG) − γ·FX − δ·fuel`, β by rolling regression; compute half-life of the spread (OU fit); trade only if half-life < 20 days and cointegration p-value < 0.1. Entry when spread z-score ≥ |2|, exit at z = 0 or 15 calendar days (time-stop). Position size = z-scaled; equal risk per pair (vol-targeted legs). Expected holding 5–10 days; ~40–60 round trips per 3-year test.

**6. Validation and risks.**
Benchmark: (i) long-only equal-weight IAG+LH basket — the dollar-neutral strategy must beat the basket's Sharpe *after costs* (10 bps round trip per leg, FX hedge ~3 bps); (ii) buy-and-hold 50/50. Reject if the strategy's post-2022 out-of-sample annualized Sharpe < basket's Sharpe + 0.3, or if the tradeable half-life drifts above 20 days (regime break — structural divergence like the German government stake or IAG's competitor-positioning change means cointegration broke down; the signal is dead until regime re-tested). Risks: liquid is thin on bad days (2016/2020 style gaps kill stop-loss), dividend timing distorts logs, borrow/lend costs on the short leg, and the economic shock ends when both stocks get re-rated together — pairs work until they don't; regime filters (skip trade if 60d spread std doubles) are mandatory.

## Question 3: Research Idea Number Three — Electricity Demand Growth as a Leading Indicator for IPPs/Data-Center Power Plays

**1. Market and scope.**
US-listed independent power producers and grid equipment names with direct data-center demand exposure: Constellation Energy (CEG), Vistra (VST), Talen Energy (TLN), NRG, plus equipment beneficiaries GE Vernova (GEV), Eaton (ETN), Vertiv (VRT), Quanta (PWR). Universe: US equities, 3–12 month horizon, quarterly rebalance, no leverage.

**2. Investment thesis.**
A structural shock to electricity demand — hyperscaler data-center build-out (OpenAI/AWS/Meta/Google/MSFT capex commitments) — lands unevenly across regions (PJM/Mid-Atlantic, ERCOT/Texas, Georgia-Virginia). IPPs in those regions re-price PPAs upward, and their earnings-per-unit grows at a rate the general market updates *quarterly*, while the demand signal (ISO load reports, interconnection queue withdrawals/approvals, substation energizations) is *monthly or weekly and public*. Thesis: aggregated forward-looking demand-use metrics at ISO level predict the *relative* revenue growth of the regionally-exposed IPPs over 3–12 months, and the slow quarterly refresh creates a persistent gap between the public data path and analyst consensus.

**3. Potential edge.**
EIA's Electric Power Monthly and ISO dashboards (PJM load forecasts, ERCOT 7-day/seasonal peak + interconnection queue XLSXs, Georgia Power's "large load" economic development pipeline) are public and free, but not consolidated per investable company; translating "PJM forecast +1.2% YoY" into "CEG's merchant fleet share of that region gives X% revenue growth per quarter" requires market-structure knowledge (which plants are in which zone, which contracts re-price when). This mapping is the moat — not the raw data.

**4. Data and sources.**

- **EIA**: Electric Power Monthly (retail sales & industrial segment incl. "commercial" = data centers), state-level kWh — monthly, ~45-day lag. *Checked access/API works.*
- **ERCOT**: hourly load + future load forecasts, Queue (large loads) — daily/weekly; PJM: Load Forecast Report, RTEP windows, data-center interconnection reports — *Checked dashboards; queue files need scraping (to verify format stability).*
- **Hyperscaler capex disclosures (10-Qs, earnings calls)** — scheduled, lagged quarterly. *Checked in yfinance/IR pages.*
- Stock data: yfinance daily. *Checked — CEG/VST/TLN/GEV etc. all liquid.*
- Cross-check: **utility sector power-demand beta** vs the definitionally unrelated sector (retail) as control. *To verify.*

**5. Research approach.**
Composite region-demand score per quarter: z-scores of (a) ISO 12-month-ahead load forecast delta, (b) queue megawatts approved in last 90 days, (c) EIA commercial sales delta, weighted by each company's disclosed capacity in that region (10-K exhibits; hand-mapped once per year). Signal = capacity-weighted regional score used to rank the universe; long top-4, benchmark STOXX US Large Cap / XLU as control. Alternatively: linear model mapping the 3 demand inputs to next-quarter revenue surprises, tested out-of-sample 2019→2026.

**6. Validation and risks.**
Benchmarks: (i) equal-weight basket of the 8 names (the signal must show *timing*, not just sector beta), (ii) XLU for pure-utility control. Reject if: adding the demand score to a plain momentum/sector-rotation model doesn't increase hit rate of *relative* quarterly ranking persistence by > 5 percentage points, or if the demand metrics don't Granger-cause company revenue surprises (F-stat at 1–2 quarters). Risks: **this is the most crowded of the 3 ideas** — institutional money already trades "power scarcity", so alpha exists mainly in *secondary* names (TLN/GEV vs CEG) and in timing earnings revisions; Y-o-Y weather noise in load data needs seasonal adjustment; regional mixes change when M&A happens (e.g. Talen sold/planned); EIA lag can hide the turn; ERCOT queue data has revisions and queue-freezing distortions; and if AI capex decelerates sharply, the whole cohort de-rates together (beta risk, not idiosyncratic).

*All three ideas deliberately differ in mechanism (public-disclosure lags / shared-factor dislocation / demand-pipeline lead), market (EU prime/mid caps, EU listed pairs, US mega/IPP), data source (TED XML, Yahoo EOD + ECB fuel, EIA/ISO files), and horizon (6–18 mo / days / 3–12 mo). None are claimed profitable; each has a defined kill switch.*
